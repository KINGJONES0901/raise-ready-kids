# Brand & Copy Notes

## Working name: Raise Ready Kids
Chosen because it says what the product does, works as a tagline ("raise ready kids"), and the initials (RRK) make a clean badge. Check domain and social handle availability before committing.

Other name options, in case you prefer one:
- **Capable Kid Co.** — plays off "raising capable kids"
- **Little Big Skills**
- **Grow Up Kit**
- **Kitchen Table Kids** — leans into the dad-at-home angle
- **Ready Set Grown**

## Positioning
The reference site's hook is "Made by a therapist." Yours is **"Made by a dad."** That's a real differentiator: parents trust other parents who are in it right now. Don't claim credentials you don't have. Your story (dad, son, got tired of hunting for good printables) is the trust element. Put a real photo of you and your son where the placeholder avatar is.

## Voice
- Plain, warm, a little blunt. Talks like a dad at the park, not a clinic.
- Short sentences. Concrete examples ("asked three times", "fifty tabs").
- Never guilt the parent. The kid is capable; the parent just needs the kit.

## What changed vs. the reference, and why
| Reference | Yours | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| "Made by a therapist" | "Made by a dad" | True for you; still a trust signal |
| "1892 verified reviews" | "Launch edition — be one of the first 1,000" | You have no reviews yet. Faking them is illegal under FTC rules and gets stores shut down. The launch framing turns "no reviews" into a reason to act. |
| "6000+ happy customers" | Omitted | Same reason. Add a real counter once you have numbers. |
| Shopify $0 checkout | Email capture form | Simpler to launch, you own the list, and it's the whole point of a free offer |
| Generic "Anniversary Sale" | "Launch week, free until midnight" | Honest urgency; the timer resets daily |
| Their product names (EMDR Kids Workbook, Polyvagal Theory for Kids) | Original names and curriculum | Don't copy their IP; their clinical names also don't fit a "made by a dad" brand |

## Price anchor
$49 crossed out to $0. The reference uses $99.99, which reads inflated for PDFs. $49 is believable for a 10-book bundle and is what you'd actually charge later.
